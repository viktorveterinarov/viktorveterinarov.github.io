1 · Overview

Course preparation

The course follows the instructor’s lecture notes. Students should already have basic knowledge of cross-sectional econometrics, including ordinary least squares, heteroskedasticity, instrumental variables, and probit, logit and Tobit models.

These concepts are introduced in the probability, statistics and econometrics courses taken in the first year of the programme.

2 · Workload

Problem sets and classes

TA sessions run from weeks 2 to 13 of the semester, with no session in week 1. A new problem set is posted on the course Moodle page almost every week, normally one week before it is due.

Five problem sets are graded. Students are strongly encouraged to complete the remaining sets as well, since the midterm and final examinations tend to be similar to the problem sets.

Study groups may collaborate, but each student submits an individual set of solutions and submissions should not be identical.

Submission and feedback

Graded sets are due on Tuesday at 6 pm in the week indicated. They are marked on a scale from 0 to 2. Two or three questions are selected at random for marking, and detailed solutions are provided afterwards.

4 · Examinations

Derivations and justification

The midterm and final include mathematical exercises requiring derivations. To receive full credit, students should justify every step and explain why the assumptions of any theorem they invoke are satisfied.

The midterm takes place in week 8. If a student misses it because of illness, a signed letter on a medical professional’s letterhead is required. There is no separate make-up midterm; its weight is transferred to the final examination.

Catch-up examination

A written catch-up examination is held after the final for students whose average is strictly below 10/20. The grade is recalculated by replacing the midterm and final contribution with:

0.4 × max(midterm, catch-up)
+ 0.4 × max(final, catch-up)

A strong catch-up result can therefore offset weak performance in both examinations. In accordance with the School of Research rules, there is no second catch-up examination except in extreme medical or personal circumstances documented within 48 hours.
